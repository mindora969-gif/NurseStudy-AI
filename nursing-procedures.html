Nursing Procedures & Patient Safety

Study frameworks for understanding procedures. Actual performance must follow current institutional policy, local scope of practice, competency assessment and direct supervision.

Before any procedure: safety sequence

  1. Verify the order, indication, patient identity using approved identifiers and allergies.
  2. Explain the procedure, obtain consent as appropriate and provide privacy.
  3. Perform hand hygiene; use appropriate PPE and aseptic technique.
  4. Prepare equipment, check integrity, expiry and compatibility.
  5. Assess the patient, perform the procedure only if trained and authorised.
  6. Monitor response, dispose of sharps safely, document and escalate concerns.

1. Injection — learning framework

Routes to understand

Intradermal, subcutaneous, intramuscular and intravenous routes differ in tissue depth, absorption characteristics, indications and risks. Route, medication, dose, site, needle/device and technique must be verified against the medication order and institutional guidance.

Safety focus: medication rights, allergy check, correct patient / medicine / dose / route / time, asepsis, sharps safety and post-administration monitoring. Never select a site or dose from a generic online note alone.

2. IV cannulation — learning framework

Concepts to revise

  • Indications, contraindications and vein assessment.
  • Hand hygiene, skin antisepsis and aseptic non-touch technique.
  • Device selection according to therapy, vein and policy.
  • Securement, labelling, patency assessment and site monitoring.
  • Recognising infiltration, extravasation, phlebitis, infection and occlusion.
  • Documentation, reassessment and timely removal when no longer indicated.
Clinical warning: IV insertion is an invasive skill. Practise only in approved simulation or under qualified clinical supervision; follow your institution's current protocol.

3. CPR / Basic Life Support

High-level response sequence

  1. Ensure scene safety and check responsiveness.
  2. Activate the emergency response system and obtain an AED / emergency equipment.
  3. Assess breathing and pulse according to your current BLS training and local protocol.
  4. If cardiac arrest is identified, begin high-quality CPR and use the AED as soon as available.
  5. Follow AED prompts and current recognised resuscitation guidelines; minimise interruptions.
  6. Continue until help takes over, signs of life return, the scene becomes unsafe or you are unable to continue.
Important: CPR details, compression rates/depths and age-specific algorithms can change and differ for adults, children and infants. Learn the current recognised guideline in a certified BLS course.

4. Procedure reflection template

For every skill, write: Purpose → Indications → Equipment → Preparation → Key safety checks → Procedure principles → Aftercare → Complications → Documentation → Patient education.
