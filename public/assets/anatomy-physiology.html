Anatomy & Physiology

Notebook-style study notes with organised headings, clinical connections, schematic diagrams, examination highlights and quick recall.

1. Introduction & organisation of the human body

Meaning

Anatomy is the study of body structures and the relationships between them. Physiology is the study of how body parts function.

Levels of organisation: chemical → cellular → tissue → organ → organ system → organism.

EXAM POINT: Homeostasis means maintaining a relatively stable internal environment despite external or internal changes.

2. Cell — structural and functional unit

Cell components

  • Plasma membrane: selectively regulates movement of substances.
  • Cytoplasm: cytosol and organelles where many metabolic reactions occur.
  • Nucleus: contains most genetic material and regulates cell activity.
  • Mitochondria: major site of ATP production in aerobic cells.
  • Ribosomes: protein synthesis.
  • Rough ER: synthesis/processing of proteins for secretion or membranes.
  • Golgi apparatus: modifies, sorts and packages proteins/lipids.
  • Lysosomes: intracellular digestion.
QUICK RECALL: Nucleus = genetic control; mitochondria = ATP; ribosome = protein; Golgi = packaging.

3. Basic tissues

Four primary tissue types

  1. Epithelial: covers surfaces, lines cavities and forms glands.
  2. Connective: supports, binds and protects; includes blood, bone, cartilage and adipose tissue.
  3. Muscle: contracts; skeletal, cardiac and smooth.
  4. Nervous: receives and transmits electrical signals; neurons and supporting glial cells.
EXAM POINT: Cardiac muscle is striated and involuntary; smooth muscle is non-striated and involuntary.

4. Skeletal system

Functions

Support, protection, movement with muscles, mineral storage (especially calcium and phosphate), and blood-cell formation in red bone marrow.

Adult human skeleton is commonly described as 206 bones (normal anatomical variation exists).

Bone classification

Long, short, flat, irregular and sesamoid bones. A typical long bone has diaphysis, epiphyses, metaphysis, periosteum, compact bone, spongy bone and medullary cavity.

5. Muscular system

Muscle types

Skeletal: voluntary movement and posture. Cardiac: myocardium; rhythmic involuntary contraction. Smooth: walls of hollow organs and blood vessels.

Muscle contraction depends on interaction of actin and myosin, regulated by calcium and ATP.

6. Nervous system

Divisions

CNS: brain and spinal cord. PNS: cranial and spinal nerves and ganglia. Functionally, the PNS includes somatic and autonomic divisions; autonomic includes sympathetic and parasympathetic branches.

EXAM POINT: A reflex arc classically includes receptor → sensory neuron → integration centre → motor neuron → effector.

7. Cardiovascular system

Heart overview

The heart has four chambers: right atrium, right ventricle, left atrium and left ventricle. Valves help maintain one-way blood flow.

Blood pathway: venae cavae → right atrium → tricuspid valve → right ventricle → pulmonary valve → pulmonary arteries → lungs → pulmonary veins → left atrium → mitral valve → left ventricle → aortic valve → aorta.

QUICK RECALL: Right heart sends deoxygenated blood to lungs; left heart sends oxygenated blood to systemic circulation.

8. Respiratory system

Air pathway

Nose / nasal cavity → pharynx → larynx → trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli. Gas exchange occurs across the alveolar-capillary membrane by diffusion.

Inspiration at rest: diaphragm contracts and descends; thoracic volume increases and air flows into lungs.

9. Digestive system

Organs & functions

Mouth: ingestion and mechanical digestion. Oesophagus: transport. Stomach: mixing and acid/enzymatic digestion. Small intestine: most digestion and nutrient absorption. Large intestine: water/electrolyte absorption and faeces formation. Liver, gallbladder and pancreas support digestion.

10. Urinary system

Kidney & nephron

Kidneys regulate fluid, electrolytes, acid-base balance and contribute to blood pressure regulation and erythropoietin production. The nephron is the functional unit: renal corpuscle, proximal tubule, loop of Henle, distal tubule and collecting duct.

Urine formation involves glomerular filtration, tubular reabsorption and tubular secretion.

11. Endocrine system

Major glands

Hypothalamus and pituitary coordinate many endocrine functions; thyroid regulates metabolic activity; parathyroids regulate calcium; adrenal glands support stress responses and electrolyte balance; pancreas produces insulin and glucagon; gonads produce sex hormones.

12. Reproductive system

Core concepts

Study male and female reproductive anatomy, gametogenesis, menstrual cycle, fertilisation, implantation and hormonal regulation. Link normal physiology to reproductive and maternal health nursing.

13. Blood & immunity

Blood components

Plasma transports substances; red blood cells carry oxygen via haemoglobin; white blood cells participate in defence; platelets support haemostasis. Haemostasis includes vascular response, platelet plug formation and coagulation.

14. Special senses

Vision, hearing, balance, taste and smell

Know the basic anatomy and sensory pathways of the eye and ear, plus olfactory, gustatory and vestibular functions. Relate structure to common assessment findings.

15. Lifespan & integrated physiology

Homeostasis and feedback

Negative feedback counteracts a change and helps stabilise a variable (for example, thermoregulation). Positive feedback amplifies a process until a specific endpoint (for example, uterine contractions during labour).
